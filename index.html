Physical systems · Simulation · Machine learning

Machine learning for physical systems, tested against reality.

I’m a physicist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. I build simulations and learned models of physical systems, infer their parameters from noisy data, and build the sensor hardware that produces the data. Throughout, the question is whether a model can be trusted: where it differs from reality, and how large its uncertainty really is.

My best-known work makes rare outcomes computable from simulation (ICLR 2025 Spotlight), the same problem as estimating how often a robot fails. I’m open to research roles in physical AI and robotics, especially in simulation, evaluation and safety.

Simulation · sim-to-real

Simulations that agree with measurements

Monte Carlo simulations of particle transport in complex 3D geometries, validated against detector data, and multi-fidelity models that learn the discrepancy between cheap and expensive simulations.

Inference · uncertainty

Physical parameters from noisy data

Bayesian models with systematic uncertainties, MCMC on NERSC supercomputers, and learned likelihood emulators that make high-dimensional inference feasible, from neutrino mass to stellar evolution.

Rare failures · evaluation

Probabilities too small to count

Backgrounds in searches for a decay whose half-life exceeds the age of the universe ten million billion times, and outcomes rarer than 1 in 10,000: estimated from simulation with uncertainties checked against independent runs.

ICLR 2025Spotlight

RESuM: a rare-event surrogate model for physics detector design

A surrogate model that learns rare-event probabilities from simulations of different fidelity, with calibrated uncertainty. In a detector-shielding study, it found a design with 66.5% less simulated background using 3.3% of the conventional compute.
