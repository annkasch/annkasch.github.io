Research scientist in physical AI, scientific simulation, and real-world sensors

01 / Experience

Research and development

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

May 2021–present

Postdoctoral Researcher · Research Scientist

  • Developed RESuM, a machine-learning method for estimating rare-event probabilities from simulation and supporting detector design. In a shielding study, it reduced simulated background by 66.5% using 3.3% of the standard compute; the work received an ICLR 2025 Spotlight.
  • Applied the method to binary black-hole population inference, reducing compute by 98% and enabling high-dimensional analysis of merger outcomes rarer than 1 in 10,000.
  • Built an LLM-based pipeline that turns incomplete detector documentation into validated 3D geometries and runnable simulations.
  • Developed neural waveform classifiers for germanium sensors and optimized inference latency for data-acquisition use.
  • Led low-noise electronics through design, cleanroom fabrication, testing, installation, and commissioning.
  • Built research software in Python and C++ and ran distributed simulation and training workloads at NERSC using Slurm, CUDA, and OpenMP.
  • Served as Early Career Representative for the LEGEND collaboration (2021–2023) and mentored students.

University of Tübingen, Germany

Jan 2014–May 2021

Postdoctoral Researcher (2019–2021) · PhD Researcher (2014–2019)

  • Contributed to electric-field and charge-drift simulations for semiconductor sensors and developed signal-processing methods to separate signal from noise.
  • Developed a Bayesian model combining heterogeneous data and systematic uncertainties to quantify individual noise sources.
  • Built C++ Monte Carlo simulations of particle transport, radioactive decays, and cosmic-ray interactions in complex 3D geometries, with validation against measurements.
  • Developed a photosensor data pipeline from data acquisition and digital signal processing to real-time noise flagging.
