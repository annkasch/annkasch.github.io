Rare events · Simulation · Machine learning

Estimating what almost never happens.

I’m a physicist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. I work on rare events: outcomes so unlikely that a simulation produces only a handful of them, from background signals in searches for neutrinoless double-beta decay, a process whose half-life exceeds the age of the universe ten million billion times over, to the stellar histories behind black-hole mergers.

I develop statistical and machine-learning methods that estimate these probabilities with far less computation, and I have built detector electronics for the experiments that search for them. The same question, how often a rare failure happens, is central to physical AI, which I also write about.

ICLR 2025 · Spotlight

RESuM: a rare-event surrogate model for physics detector design

A surrogate model that learns rare-event probabilities from simulations of different fidelity, with calibrated uncertainty. In a detector-shielding study, it found a design with 66.5% less simulated background using 3.3% of the conventional compute.
