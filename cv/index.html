Experience

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory

May 2021 – Present

Postdoctoral Researcher, Research Scientist

  • Made rare-event design optimization affordable. Built RESuM, an open-source ML surrogate that learns intractable event probabilities directly from simulation and combines physics models of different fidelity to give calibrated, uncertainty-aware predictions. Used it to optimize detector shielding in the search for a nuclear decay with a half-life longer than the age of the universe, cutting rare noise from cosmic-ray showers by 66.5% at 3.3% of standard compute. Adopted by multiple scientific collaborations. Paper | Code | Video | Project page
  • Replaced a slow simulator with a fast ML stand-in. Repurposed RESuM as a likelihood emulator for binary black-hole merger outcomes rarer than 1 in 10,000, showing it works beyond design optimization. It cut compute by 98% and enabled the first full high-dimensional parameter inference, making population-synthesis studies of stellar evolution feasible. Project page
  • Automated the step from engineering documents to runnable simulation. Built an LLM agent that turns sparse, incomplete multimodal detector documentation into physically consistent 3D geometries and executable simulations, combining knowledge graphs, hierarchical spatial representations, and automated geometric validation.
  • Brought ML toward real-time sensor decisions. Developed and benchmarked deep-learning models that classify waveforms (time-series signals) from germanium semiconductor sensors to reject noise, and optimized their inference latency for integration into the data acquisition system.
  • Owned hardware end to end. Led the design, cleanroom fabrication (thin-film sputtering, photolithography, trace etching), testing, installation, and commissioning of ultra-low-noise electronics and data acquisition systems. Project page
  • Built reproducible research software and scaled it on supercomputers. Wrote research code in Python and C++ (PyTorch, TensorFlow) and ran distributed simulation and training pipelines at NERSC, the U.S. Department of Energy’s national supercomputing center, using Slurm, CUDA, and OpenMP.
  • Led and mentored. Early Career Representative for the international LEGEND collaboration (2021–2023). Mentored students and presented at international conferences and ML workshops.

  • Simulated semiconductor sensor physics. Contributed to electric-field and charge-drift simulations of how signals form in semiconductor sensors; developed signal-processing algorithms to separate signal from noise.
